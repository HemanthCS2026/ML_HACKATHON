## 3. Viva Defense Summary (Quick Reference)

### Q: Why do you need metric projection?
**A:** Latitude and longitude are angular coordinates on a sphere. 1 degree of longitude gets narrower as latitude increases (`dx = dlon * 111139 * cos(lat)`). Euclidean distance directly on degrees gives distorted numbers. We convert to local meters centered at the trip median so distances and speeds are physically accurate.

### Q: Why not check all road segments in candidate generation?
**A:** Some road networks have 20,000 to 300,000 links. Checking all of them for 2,500 GPS points would mean ~500 million distance calculations per trip. We build a KDTree on road nodes, find the 20 nearest nodes in `O(log N)` time, and check only their incident arcs. This gives 96.51% candidate recall while running in milliseconds.

### Q: How does your HMM calculate transition probabilities?
**A:** We use the Newson-Krumm exponential model: `P(transition) = (1/beta) * exp(-|d_network - d_gps| / beta)`. If two candidate segments are directly connected in the road graph, `d_network` is close to `d_gps`, so the transition probability is high. If they are disconnected, we run a short Dijkstra search; if no path exists, a heavy penalty is applied. For large GPS dropouts (> 10s), the penalty is relaxed so the model doesn't break on missing data.

### Q: How does the two-laptop setup work?
**A:** 
- **Laptop 1 (Server):** Runs `python api.py` on port 8000. Holds the models, road graph, and ground truth route files. Prints request logs in terminal.
- **Laptop 2 (Client UI):** Runs `streamlit run app.py`. Has no ground truth data. Sends trip ID to Laptop 1 over HTTP, receives the predicted route, and displays it on the Folium map.
